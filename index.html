How it plays out

A correction hedge. You buy the 5%-below put and sell a 15%-below put, so the sold leg recovers part of the premium. Fully protected between −5% and −15%; below −15% you fall with the market again, just better off by the band. No cap on upside. Cheaper than the outright put precisely because it sells away the crash protection — the wrong structure for the scenario this tool is built to catch, the right one when lenders are calm and the move is a dip.

How it plays out

Plain insurance. You pay the premium; below the strike the put pays you dollar for dollar, with no limit on how far — it behaves the same at −15% and at −50%. Above the strike it expires worthless and the premium is the cost of having been wrong. No cap on your upside. The real cost is time: rolling this every month is roughly 12–13% of the sleeve a year, which is why the posture only points here when the odds are elevated and the price is fair.
